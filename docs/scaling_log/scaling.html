7 · Policy heads

Every action prior trained so far (9 heads, 5 readable on one common set), with what each was trained on. Separate heads are the #419 prior: a small MLP on the same 833 root inputs; joint heads sit on a value net’s trunk (#425). Root rows are decision roots from the mover’s seat with the action the search played and the ballot it ranked; split is the slice of the value split’s hash order they came from (test is [0, 0.1), val [0.1, 0.2), train the rest), so only heads trained on [0.2, 1) are held out from the common set. The common set is 15,517 rows on the 1,018 test deals; wide means more than 100 legal actions (2,340 rows). Listwise CE is the cross-entropy of the search’s chosen action against the ballot the search ranked, the scoring rule for “which action would the search play”; the BCE column is the 54-card multi-hot training loss and is not comparable across heads; recall is the played action inside the head’s top-k of the stored candidates on wide rows, and the four strata are top-64 on exhaustive 101–1k, partial 101–1k, 1k–10k and 10k+ (deal counts 532 / 497 / 455 / 67), all on the common set; a head’s own-split numbers, where quoted, sit in its note. Point differences between heads are read with the paired deal bootstrap (#425 4.2): “non-inferiority not shown” and “inferior” are different verdicts. Top-256 is 0.999 for every trained head and 0.996 for the untrained one, because records keep at most 256 candidates: the consumer’s operating point (256 of thousands per world) is invisible offline, which is why the prior arm played M1’s lines in the screens. Value cost is the outcome head’s val_ce against its matched twin.
