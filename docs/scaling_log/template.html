Records scale linearly at 146.2 per cluster (sd 0.44 across seven corpora spanning 18×). A cluster is one deal played twice with seats mirrored, so 73.1 records per round. Run I is the exception at 135.9, about 7% low, because a shortlist that prunes harder ends a round in fewer recorded decisions. Runs K, L and M are the first corpora carrying bury_records > 0, and M also carries the full-legal score vector; they use a different teacher, so folding them in would move mixture and quantity together. 144,000 clusters is the largest clean corpus. The runJS block is different in kind from everything above it: its teacher is not production MC but JS-M1 served as one package (value net plus its own policy head as the admission prior at threshold 1,000 / top 256, hybrid bury) — the release-28 recipe. These are the generation corpora: games the current best net played against itself, recorded so the next net can train on them. runJS1 alone trained gen-1 and is 7.9% of that net’s policy corpus — that is the POLICY-row increment, 1.74M of 22.04M, not a ratio of the value-record counts in this table; at 138.4 records per cluster it sits about 5% below the 146.2 rate for the same reason run I does. runJS1–3 together trained gen-2 and are 20.5% of its policy corpus: 48,000 clusters and 6,643,492 records. runJS1–5 are training gen-3 now at 30.0% — the top of the ladder pre-registered on #421 — and that run is the first of the series to be warm-started from JS-M1 rather than trained from fresh random weights, which every earlier generation was. runJS6 onward feed a fourth generation, not this one. They are not part of the 144k corpus, and folding them in would move teacher and quantity together.

8 · Policy heads

Every action prior trained so far ({{N_POLICY}} heads, {{N_POLICY_COMMON}} readable on one common set), with what each was trained on. Separate heads are the #419 prior: a small MLP on the same 833 root inputs; joint heads sit on a value net’s trunk (#425). Root rows are decision roots from the mover’s seat with the action the search played and the ballot it ranked; split is the slice of the value split’s hash order they came from (test is [0, 0.1), val [0.1, 0.2), train the rest), so only heads trained on [0.2, 1) are held out from the common set. The common set is 15,517 rows on the 1,018 test deals; wide means more than 100 legal actions (2,340 rows). Listwise CE is the cross-entropy of the search’s chosen action against the ballot the search ranked, the scoring rule for “which action would the search play”; the BCE column is the 54-card multi-hot training loss and is not comparable across heads; recall is the played action inside the head’s top-k of the stored candidates on wide rows, and the four strata are top-64 on exhaustive 101–1k, partial 101–1k, 1k–10k and 10k+ (deal counts 532 / 497 / 455 / 67), all on the common set; a head’s own-split numbers, where quoted, sit in its note. Point differences between heads are read with the paired deal bootstrap (#425 4.2): “non-inferiority not shown” and “inferior” are different verdicts. Top-256 is 0.999 for every trained head and 0.996 for the untrained one, because records keep at most 256 candidates: the consumer’s operating point (256 of thousands per world) is invisible offline, which is why the prior arm played M1’s lines in the screens. Head alone vs SmartBot (perfect-info) is the head playing by itself — argmax over its card log-odds, no value net and no rollouts — on mirrored deals, signed level per round with a paired bootstrap interval, under perfect information: the harness fed the live round’s world block, all four hands and the burial (Codex audit, 2026-09-19), so it is a privileged diagnostic, comparable between heads and NOT public playing strength. No column in this table measures the goal the policy line exists for; every other column measures the admission prior’s shortlist coverage. The public-information series on the serving-time sampled-world scheme is the read that will. The figures are the 8,000-deal read on fresh seeds; a 2,000-deal discovery read on a different seed window said the opposite and did not survive, swapping the two models’ places, so only the confirmed column is quoted. Value cost is the outcome head’s val_ce against its matched twin.
