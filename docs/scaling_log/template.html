Records scale linearly at 146.2 per cluster (sd 0.44 over seven corpora spanning 18×). A cluster is one deal played twice with seats mirrored. These are the generation corpora: games the best net played against itself so the next net can train on them. runJS1 trained gen-1 and is 7.9% of its policy rows (1.74M of 22.04M — a policy-row ratio, not a ratio of this table’s value counts). runJS1–3 trained gen-2 at 20.5%. 7 · Policy heads

Every action prior trained so far ({{N_POLICY}} heads, {{N_POLICY_COMMON}} readable on one common set). Separate heads are the #419 prior, a small MLP on the same 833 root inputs; joint heads sit on a value net’s trunk (#425). The common set is 15,517 rows on 1,018 test deals; wide means over 100 legal actions (2,340 rows). Only heads trained on the [0.2, 1) hash slice are held out from it. Listwise CE scores “which action would the search play” against the ballot it ranked. BCE is the 54-card training loss and is not comparable across heads. Recall is the played action inside the head’s top-k on wide rows, top-64 across four strata (532 / 497 / 455 / 67 deals). Differences use the paired deal bootstrap: “non-inferiority not shown” and “inferior” are different verdicts. Top-256 is 0.999 for every trained head and 0.996 for the untrained one, because records keep at most 256 candidates. The consumer’s real operating point is invisible offline, which is why the prior arm played M1’s lines in the screens.
