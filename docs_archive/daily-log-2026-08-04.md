results, and a much more trustworthy path to the next bot:

- **N=30 MC beat N=10 MC** by `+0.262 +/- 0.154` paired signed level utility
  over 504 preregistered fresh seed clusters. A true same-policy null control

  pinned to the pre-action-fix executable at `e3aeec1`; current `main` still
  needs a fresh confirmation before deployment.
- **No learned policy has beaten MC.** `rl-override-v11pair` remains the best
  learned result: confirmed 57.7% against SmartBot, extremely fast, and
  plausibly near MC in a large screen. Its 4,880-round MC comparison was
  unseeded because factories swallowed their seeds, so it proves neither
  superiority nor formal parity.
- **The sampler P0 gate closed for validity and bounded support.** The rewrite
  fixed real hidden-world construction bugs and passed an independent
  rules-derived validator: 38,399 accepted worlds over 1,600 reservoir states,
  zero invalid, plus exhaustive recovery of every legal world in 120/120 toy
  states. Posterior probability fidelity is still open.
- **Submitted-action semantics were repaired in both Python and Cython.** A
  six-card tied-trump witness exposed order-dependent decomposition and missing
  physical tractor choices. The fixed kernels are invariant over 30,936
  bounded reorderings, warm/cold caches, and real `Round.play` successors.
- **The high-N corpus became useful by being demoted from “oracle” to
  diagnostic.** It found a lead-focused opportunity and exposed a label/deploy
  mismatch, but no strength win. Better offline fit failed to transfer online.
- **The ballot problem is now localized.** Structured omission is 51.2% on
  leads and only 0.9% on follows. Naively adding the missing singles did not
  help; generation, selection, and rollout allocation must be tested as
  separate layers.
- **The 512-state lead pilot became a real experiment rather than a script.**
  Its action semantics, hidden-information boundary, fold independence,
  equal-work arms, retained-vector arithmetic, clustered aggregation, and raw
  deep-state capture were all made explicit and fail-closed. Scoring remains
  correctly frozen at 0/512 until the balanced state artifact exists.
- **Frontend reconnect/join/takeover behavior became an explicit protocol.**
  Resume tokens, reservation grace, bot cover, generation-scoped teardown,
  room-scoped chat, and a single client connection-intent decision are now
  covered by adversarial tests.
- **Simplification removed multiple sources of experimental truth.** Six duel
  runners became one evaluation library; ballot identity became executable;
  deep capture sharding stopped doing roughly 7/8 disposable work; the working
  docs were separated from durable synthesis.

The most important meta-result is that correctness, action identity, sampling,
evaluation, and state distribution are part of the ML algorithm. Several
apparently positive results disappeared when those contracts were enforced.
That is progress, not lost work: compute is now much less likely to reinforce a
measurement bug.

## 1. ML and RL findings

### Model lineage was clarified: v7 through v13 are experiments, not a ladder

| model | what it actually tested | what survived |
|---|---|---|
| **v7/v7w** | Lower-noise N=30 distillation labels; warm start from v6. | Useful historical initializer and leaf control. No verified edge over MC after evaluator hardening. |
| **v8 A/B** | Larger corpus and a trainer fix; B aligned the policy target with the deployed +5 baseline margin while A retained raw-value targets. | B raised held-out teacher-choice agreement from about 43% to 62.5%, proving the objective mismatch mattered. The online anchors were pre-seeding-fix and did not establish strength. Better imitation is not automatically a better bot. |
| **v9 warm/scratch** | New hybrid-teacher corpus, initialization control, longer training, and one expert-iteration/flywheel turn. | Warm and scratch were indistinguishable; both weakened after their best epoch; the new value head did not improve the leaf. This pipeline failed, not expert iteration in general. |
| **v10res** | First residual/override checkpoint. | Implementation-invalid as a test of residual learning: independent-row rather than pairwise loss, train/play ballot mismatch, head mismatch, and near-zero override rate. Reject the checkpoint, not the hypothesis. |

| **v12** | No model used this number. | Intentionally absent; do not invent a missing failure. |
| **v13abs** | Absolute 240-world `Q^Heuristic(s,a)` labels from the high-N corpus, inverse-variance weighting, deployed as a truncated-rollout leaf. | Offline RMSE improved `0.1052 -> 0.0699` and stored-ballot regret improved, but online v13-v7 was `-0.028 +/- 0.185`. The A-vs-B and v10-v11 stories point to the same conclusion: apparent model
capacity was not the main bottleneck. Exact agreement among loss, action set,
baseline, threshold, perspective, and inference role mattered more.

### The confirmed determinization result—and its boundary

The old sampler produced mixed evidence. Its fresh confirmation blocks gave
N=30 minus N=10 `+0.101 +/- 0.150`, and 14 zero-world fallbacks contaminated
the dose interpretation. After the sampler rewrite, the preregistered final
block at seeds 99,000,000+ produced:

| contrast | paired signed level utility | n |
|---|---:|---:|
| **N=30 minus N=10** | **+0.262 +/- 0.154** | 504 clusters |
| N=30 minus true null control | +0.310 +/- 0.153 | 504 |
| null minus N=10 | -0.048 +/- 0.162 | 504 |

The null was the same N=10 policy with an independent RNG stream. Its flat
result is what attributes the gain to search dose rather than to the harness.
This makes `mc-strong` the version-pinned strength incumbent. It does **not**
prove an RL gain, posterior correctness, or that current `main`—whose action
semantics and tractor ballot changed later—has the same edge.

Primary promotion metric is now paired signed **level utility by deal seed**.
Round win rate remains descriptive because it discards the size and direction
of level progress; a final product candidate still needs mirrored full-game
level progression.

### Important negative results and retractions

- **Root-prior racing was refuted.** An early result appeared to beat MC, but
  did not replicate. The random-prune control ranked at or above the v11 prior,
  so pruning/compute redistribution—not learned knowledge—explained the screen.
- **The v11-vs-MC “seeded” blocks were not seeded.** Registry/call-site lambdas
  accepted `seed=` and silently dropped it. The 51.1% over 4,880 rounds is a
  useful screen, not a confirmation or non-inferiority result.
- **V3 lead widening was not confirmed:** `+0.065 +/- 0.144`; its equal-slot
  random-fill control was nominally higher. More candidates without better
  selection and enough worlds can make search resolution worse.
- **The 0.005 v11 override threshold was not confirmed:** direct paired
  contrast `-0.032 +/- 0.184` over 250 clusters despite looking better offline.
- **v13 absolute-Q leaf was not confirmed.** It fit old labels substantially
  better and played no better.
- **DMC2/AWAC conclusions were invalidated at the target level.** Defender
  returns subtracted an attacker-perspective oracle without flipping the
  oracle sign. The recipe was also neither faithful Suphx privileged-policy
  training nor faithful DouZero direct-Q. The spread alarm worked and should be
  retained; the two halted runs do not reject those RL families.

### Literature synthesis changed the architecture plan

AlphaGo/AlphaZero contribute the division of labor—policy for proposals,
search for improvement, value for truncation—but not an ordinary private-state
MCTS transplant. Shengji has four seats, hidden hands, partnership signaling,
and action-dependent information. A private observation has no single
strategy-independent scalar value.

The resulting plan is:

1. Near term: calibrated belief worlds, a broad lead generator, a learned or
   structured selector, a minimum common-world rollout floor, then sequential
   root allocation.
2. Medium term: train lead/follow (and eventually declaration/bury) separately;
   use v11-like models as proposers/rankers on the exact ballot they saw.
3. Faithful RL probes: one Suphx-style privileged-policy curriculum and one
   from-scratch, role-conditioned DouZero-style direct-Q microbaseline, each
   with immutable actor snapshots and target-symmetry tests.
4. Long term: public-history belief state plus calibrated hand ranges and a
   fixed portfolio of continuation policies, borrowing representations from
   Libratus/Pluribus/ReBeL rather than borrowing guarantees that do not transfer.
5. AutoGo-style automation comes only after synchronous collect/train/evaluate
   is reproducible. Automation may schedule preregistered work; it may not
   invent metrics or promote checkpoints.

## 2. Corpus and ballot findings

### What the high-N work bought

The completed Air run captured 20,000 fixed states, evaluated each offered
action over 240 shared worlds, and performed 37.1M candidate evaluations. The
analysis artifact contains those 20,000 plus a retained 845-state Mini partial,
for 20,845 replayable states. Shared worlds and paired differences made close
actions much easier to compare than independent labels.

This was a major methodological improvement—compute spent **vertically** on
fewer fixed states and every counterfactual action—but not a clean oracle:

- labels used the old non-strict sampler;
- the action universe was the old finite ballot;
- the target was raw-point `Q^Heuristic(s,a)`, not level utility or generic
  value;
- the selected maximum and its reported gap used the same 240 worlds;
- about 90% of states were early relative to the deployed leaf distribution.

Measured directionally, leads showed a 2.959-point selected-max gap versus
1.006 for follows. The unadjusted “greater than two SE” rates were 49% and 16%.
Those are not causal effect sizes: lead states also offered 11.08 candidates
on average versus 6.14, so winner's curse inflates the contrast. The correct
next estimator uses disjoint proposal and report worlds.

The durable corpus conclusion is narrower and more useful: **lead decisions
are the highest-information place to investigate, while offline regret from
this artifact cannot promote a policy.** It has failed to predict online
strength three times.

### Late-state distribution was repaired as raw data

A separate 12,000-state supplement at ply 15-57 increased ply >=20 coverage
from 844 to 9,237 states, a 10.9x increase. Its raw states are useful; its old
N=240 labels are contract-dirty. This distinction—state reservoir versus
teacher labels—was made explicit across the documentation.

The existing DEV set still contained only three trick-12+ lead deals, so it
could not support the registered early/mid/late pilot. That led to a new raw
deep-lead reservoir: 3 splits x tricks 12-19 x 2 leader roles x 16 states =
768, with DEV/CALIB/REPORT assigned before play and REPORT never inspected.

### Ballot diagnosis: sourcing exists, widening is not enough

The corrected static audit replayed 12,340 DEV states with zero rebuild errors:

| surface | structured actions omitted by deployed ballot |
|---|---:|
| **leads** | **51.2%** |
| follows | **0.9%** |

The remaining structured gap is almost entirely lead singles. Follows are
effectively solved at this diagnostic boundary. The first audit incorrectly
counted unrelated pair throws as tractors and reported 54.0%; the corrected
engine-derived definition gives 51.2%.

Fixing tied-code tractor enumeration changed the actual action sets but did
not move the omission fraction, because the same helper feeds both deployed
and reference ballots. That was another useful correction: shared-code audits
can be self-consistently wrong, so the reference must be inspected for common
omissions.

The design implication is a three-layer ballot:

1. **Generate** a structurally rich legal universe.
2. **Select** a fixed-size contextual subset with explicit archetype quotas or
   a learned proposer.
3. **Evaluate/allocate** enough common worlds to resolve close candidates.

Coverage alone is not the objective. The selector must beat random fill,
quota-only, and “MC-more” work controls on untouched worlds.

## 3. Correctness advances

### Hidden-world sampler rewrite and certification

The old allocator shuffled unseen cards and greedily filled seats. It could
dead-end despite a feasible real deal, silently lowering search dose or falling
back to candidate 0. The rewrite chooses suit-count matrices before cards,
orders the most constrained suit first, enumerates splits with forward
checking, then assigns physical cards under pair/run caps.

Certification found and fixed several defects rather than merely blessing the
new code:

- a declarer's pinned shown card could combine with a sampled copy into a pair
  the history proved impossible;
- remaining pair caps were interpreted incorrectly after pair/tractor follows;
- tractor run-length caps were recorded but never consumed;
- a slice/index mutation could deal a third copy of a two-copy card;
- banker kitty cards had previously been removed from `Memory.unseen` and then
  removed a second time by MC, disabling banker search through zero worlds.

The P0 certificate proves accepted-world validity against independently
derived public constraints and support on a bounded exhaustive family. It does
not prove posterior probabilities. `_splits()` does not yet weight count
matrices by their number of concrete completions, and capped card filling is
not uniform. More samples reduce variance around that biased distribution;
they do not erase the bias.

### Action identity, decomposition, and compiled parity

Equal effective levels are not equal physical actions. Under tied trump-rank
cards, different code choices can leave different successor hands. A six-card
`C7 C7 D7 D7 H7 H7` witness showed that cached decomposition and tractor
generation depended on input order and could omit a successor-distinct action.

The repair canonicalized decomposition inputs in both kernels, enumerated all
bounded physical tied-code tractor choices, preserved efficient exact-order
tractor memo keys where values are canonical, and added pure/Cython parity and
real `Round.play` regressions. Production MC candidate generation was also
canonicalized so incidental hand-list order could not flip a decision. Old
end-to-end failures at seeds 772006 and 772045 are now preserved tests.

### Deep-capture admission bug found by the real fleet run

The first 8-shard deep capture from `8a6c2af` ended with four complete shards
and four aborts. The abort was not an illegal world entering search:
`rejected_worlds` means strict MCBot safely refused the proposed invalid world.
But the affected decision used fewer than N=30 worlds, so the trajectory was
not eligible for the registered reservoir.

Schema v2 now makes the intended policy executable:

- a rejected proposal excludes the **entire deal**, records observed counters,
  rejection cause, and excluded-deal count, then continues scanning;
- accepted-trajectory sampler counters must remain exactly zero;
- a zero-world heuristic fallback or actually used impossible world still
  aborts the shard;
- merge validates both accepted and observed accounting.

Known seed 92,000,381 reproduces two rejected worlds at CALIB trick 12 with no
zero/impossible-world event. Full pure and compiled suites each passed 253
tests (2 skipped), and two clean compiled eight-shard smokes were byte-identical.
The old v1 artifacts were quarantined together; fresh v2 raw capture is GO,
while scored pilot work remains at 0/512.

### House rules are no longer mistaken for defects

The comparison against rbtying and other Shengji rulesets produced an explicit
house-rule table. Current deliberate choices include kitty multiplier
`2 x final-play card count`, different-suit self-overcall, top-component throw
comparison, and the chosen partial-tractor follow obligation. These are now
documented divergences, not bugs for a future cleanup to “fix.”

## 4. Evaluation and experimental integrity

The evaluator was a major source of false conclusions. Today closed the most
important boundaries:

- every policy factory now propagates distinct deterministic seat seeds through
  the actual constructor boundary; unsupported constructors fail rather than
  silently dropping the seed;
- the main evaluator requires a clean tree, strict sampler mode, an explicit
  control, an exclusive output path, and a declared promotion bar;
- arm, control, and reference use paired deal seeds; aggregation clusters by
  deal seed and reports the direct arm-minus-control contrast;
- manifests bind git, actor, ballot, config, sampler counters, and work;
- shard aggregation refuses duplicates, unequal labels, mixed schema/commit,
  dirty protocol counters, or missing states;
- a true null control distinguishes a model/search effect from pipeline noise;
- six overlapping duel scripts were deleted in favor of
  `shengji/evaluation.py` plus a thin CLI and contract tests.

Other failures caught today justify the strictness: Python's randomized
`hash()` made supposedly identical runs diverge across processes; run IDs
collided under parallel shards; one aborted partial contaminated an aggregate;
and maintenance cleanup deleted confirmation artifacts mid-run. The cleanup
rule is now “delete only artifacts with a committed supersession manifest,”
not “delete things that look stale.”

## 5. The lead-ballot pilot became actionable

The planned 512-state pilot now separates proposal quality, search dose, and
evaluation rather than bundling them:

- current ballot;
- V3 structured widening;
- same-slot random fill;
- `MC-more` as a work/dose control;
- fixed-size contextual/quota selection;
- full-universe/high-compute reference.

Proposal, oracle, and report folds use independent hash-derived streams.
Hidden hands cannot leak into proposal construction. Mutation is explicitly
shape-preserving for ADD/REMOVE/REPLACE and checked against independent
small-hand oracles plus live-hand sweeps. Arms retain raw per-world report
vectors, and aggregation re-derives both regret and mean rather than trusting
summary fields. Candidate-world work is measured rather than inferred from a
call count.

The state split is deal-grouped and role/band aware. Because the original
corpus could not supply late leads, raw deep capture was inserted before
scoring rather than quietly changing the estimand. This is why “0/512” is a
success condition today: the experiment was prevented from producing a clean-
looking answer to the wrong question.

## 6. Performance, fleet use, and simplification

- The compiled engine remains about **3.42x faster per MC round** than pure
  Python on the measured baseline, with byte-identical seeded histories and
  randomized differential tests. Today extended the semantic parity boundary
  through the tied-code/action fixes rather than trading correctness for speed.
- An Air/Mini preflight caught a separately built Cython binary digest mismatch
  caused by Apple clang 21 versus clang 17. The fleet did not mix artifacts:
  Air now receives Mini's verified `.so`, and every shard binds that digest.
- Deep capture switched from seed-residue sharding to disjoint pre-play
  `(split, trick)` ownership. Each deal is simulated by at most one worker;
  the old scheme would have captured every cell on every worker and discarded
  roughly 7/8 at merge.
- Six duel runners collapsed into one tested evaluation library, reducing
  duplicated seeding, aggregation, and manifest logic. `t3_gate_screen.py`
  remains separate because it is a genuinely different screen.
- `fleet_status` now works from any current directory and fails loudly instead
  of silently reporting no work. `JOBS.md` was repeatedly reconciled against
  real processes, and stopped/invalid jobs were not allowed to consume idle
  fleet capacity merely to improve utilization.
- Documentation was simplified into clear ownership: policy synthesis,
  research/model history, correctness, active backlog, compute ledger, and
  archived chronology. `AI_POLICIES.md` now has a high-signal synthesis above
  a working-notes fold.

Remaining high-ROI performance work is incremental `Memory`, int-native hands,
more compiled rollout orchestration, and reusable clone state. GPU inference is
not the bottleneck for the small current value net; action enumeration and
encoding dominate the value-leaf path.

## 7. Frontend and multiplayer lifecycle

The joining/rejoining/takeover/chat surface initially felt complex because it
encoded several independent notions—seat identity, connection lifetime, bot
control, explicit user intent, and chat history—without naming them. Today
made those concepts explicit:

- an opaque resume token identifies the exact seat; display names are not
  identity;
- connection generations prevent a stale socket teardown from disconnecting a
  newly resumed session;
- a dropped human seat is reserved during a grace window, then becomes visibly
  claimable; a bot covers turns without destroying the human seat;
- reconnecting after bot cover receives the real current hand and resumes
  control; takeover plays exactly once per turn;
- seat races return `seat_unavailable` rather than silently moving a player to
  the other team; reserved seats return `seat_reserved`;
- the client has one pure connection-intent decision: invite link, then current
  explicit action, then saved resume. The WebSocket layer no longer secretly
  rejoins from local storage and races the UI;
- chat is room-scoped, snapshot-then-live, monotonic-ID deduplicated, survives
  reconnect, and cannot leak into the next room;
- readiness ignores disconnected humans, room cleanup is cancelled by a valid
  reconnect, bot takeover/handback is announced, and explicit leave clears the
  saved room;
- the engine now sends play shape, so the audio layer no longer calls two
  unrelated pairs a tractor.

The tests now cover refresh, stale sockets, same-name/token behavior, grace-
window reservation, two clients racing for a seat, mid-game bot claims,
takeover and handback, room switching, chat duplication/loss, and no-hang
cleanup. Remaining work is a concurrent-load soak and eventual API-module
splitting only after these tests remain the contract.

## 8. Durable conclusions from the day

1. **Best verified strength is still search, not RL.** The N=30
   result is real but version-pinned. No learned policy has beaten MC.
2. **v11pair's key insight is objective alignment.** Use it as a direct
   override or proposal prior on its exact ballot, not as an absolute leaf.
3. **The immediate strength target is lead proposal plus MC allocation.** The
   measured gap is in leads; naive widening is already a negative result.
4. **A better label is only better for its named estimand.** v13 demonstrated
   that accurate `Q^Heuristic` on early, old-ballot states can be useless at a
   later, different-ballot leaf.
5. **Sampling has three separate gates:** legal validity, support, and
   probability fidelity. P0 closed the first two; the third still limits value
   calibration and final promotion.
6. **Raw states, counterfactual teacher labels, and episodic RL returns are
   different artifact types.** They must not be mixed under one “training
   data” label.
7. **More compute is earned by a frozen question.** State distribution,
   ballot, utility, sampler, continuation policy, control, and promotion bar
   must be fixed before the fleet scales the run.
8. **Hidden-information search needs beliefs and policy-consistent
   continuation.** Ordinary independent-world MCTS plus a private scalar leaf
   is not the main roadmap.
9. **Retractions are first-class results.** Root racing, V3, threshold tuning,
   v13, and the old v11-vs-MC interpretation narrowed the search space and
   exposed exactly which controls were missing.
