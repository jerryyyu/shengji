## Capture-v3 failure and the purchased lesson

The failure was not random compute noise. A legal throw can leave every player
with one card before the engine's phase classifier reaches `late`. The v3
selector treated “one card each” as sufficient for the exact-late stratum,
while its frozen quota and replay validator also required phase `late`. Seeds
`170002101` and `170007422` exposed the disagreement. The controller therefore
refused before publishing those shards, later waves never started, and none of
the six partial artifacts can enter labels.

The broader lesson is that an evidence namespace change must be propagated
through every consumer. Capture-v4 would otherwise have succeeded and then
been rejected by the label controller's hard-bound v3 packet and marker. The
v4/v5/v6 propagation proved every consumer can refuse a stale parent; v7 must
be rebound through those same heads before labels, checkpoints or REPORT
evidence exist.

## Label-v1 terminal failure bought a statistical correction

After exact packet PASS, Mini consumed the global v1 receipt and the first
eight scheduled shard slots. Two shards completed and six terminally refused:
971/1,024 rows completed and 53 refused. The untouched slots are
`1,2,3,6,7,9,11,13`; they will never run. No second wave or aggregate exists,
and no partial or REPORT utility was inspected. Receipt external SHA is
`0c3d7ea0…adc1c`; all global/shard locks and eight artifacts are preserved.

Plain-English motivation: selection and report folds need independent random
draws from the same hidden-card posterior. They do not need distinct realized
deals. V1 deleted duplicates within each fold and any deal seen by an earlier
fold. In late states with only a handful of possible hidden deals, it could
draw successfully 10,240 times yet retain as few as 45 unique worlds. One row
filled all 256 selection worlds but retained only 122 report worlds because
8,223 valid report draws overlapped selection. Every sampler failure counter
was zero—the uniqueness rule itself caused the refusal.

Worse, deleting repeats made frequent worlds count only once, flattening the
posterior distribution. This was therefore not a request to raise an attempt
cap. The correct repair is sampling with replacement: repeated worlds keep
their probability mass, while deterministic domain-separated RNG streams keep
selection/report/audit draws independent.
