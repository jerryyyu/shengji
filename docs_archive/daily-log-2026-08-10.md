## Capture-v3 failure and the purchased lesson

The failure was not random compute noise. A legal throw can leave every player
with one card before the engine's phase classifier reaches `late`. The v3
selector treated “one card each” as sufficient for the exact-late stratum,
while its frozen quota and replay validator also required phase `late`. Seeds
`170002101` and `170007422` exposed the disagreement. The controller therefore
refused before publishing those shards, later waves never started, and none of
the six partial artifacts can enter labels.

The broader lesson is that an evidence namespace change must be propagated
through every consumer. Capture-v4 would otherwise have succeeded and then
been rejected by the label controller's hard-bound v3 packet and marker. The
v4/v5/v6 propagation proved every consumer can refuse a stale parent; v7 must
be rebound through those same heads before labels, checkpoints or REPORT
evidence exist.

## Capture-v5/v6 canonical-source repairs

The v4 review correctly proved its phase fix, but it did not cover a different
representation boundary. The model encodes a hand as a multiset; the Stage-C
proposal source was partially walking the engine's incidental hand-list order.
Reversing only that list preserved the encoded state while changing novel
action order in 80/300 audited play states, the deterministic random play in
1/300, a structured follow proposal at seed `170000133`, and the random bury
at seed `170000000`.

V5 sorted exhaustive actions by canonical action key, evaluated structured
follow over a temporarily sorted hand with unconditional restoration, and
sampled random buries from a sorted hand. A later audit found its incumbent
SmartBot bury still inherited hand order at an equal-valued cutoff. V6 fixed
that last boundary by sorting during incumbent and structured construction and
restoring the exact original list object. Claude passed it at `8d6ce71`;
the complete capture/rebind slice and 300-play/50-bury parity soak were clean.
