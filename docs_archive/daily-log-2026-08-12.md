## Key synthesized learnings

1. **The pair observation exposed a world-model seam, not a root-ballot seam.**
   Production's root bot already uses public Memory and offered Jerry's `D5
   D5` in VXVS. Ordinary Monte Carlo continuations use `HeuristicBot`, which
   forgets exhausted higher pairs. Changing only that continuation was strongly
   positive in bounded exact endgames.
2. **Recurrence is not human-preference evidence.** The live-log trigger was
   common enough to matter, but humans and bots chose the promoted pair at
   nearly the same observed rate (~20%). The causal evidence is the fresh
   treatment/null exact screen; the census only sizes natural traffic.
3. **A good local continuation can still be diluted by conservative root
   search.** The pair rule activated in 17/24 natural searches but changed only
   one root move. That keeps the hypothesis alive while warning that a uniform
   whole-game screen may need substantial sample—or a tighter natural-dose
   estimand—to see it.
4. **S4 needs prospective accumulation, not retroactive pooling.** Its two old
   estimates can motivate a new sample size but cannot be combined after the
   fact as confirmatory evidence. The two-look successor fixes this before any
   fresh outcome exists.
5. **S6's first HOLD was useful and cheap.** Freezing a Mini-compatible packet
   for an Air job would have made the capacity result nonportable. The v2 packet
   now binds the machine, interpreter and branch-built compiled engine before
   any work begins.
6. **Two-tier rigor is now explicit.** The pair exact test was fast,
   reproducible exploration. Whole-game strength still requires fresh paired
   populations, adequate power, external review and literal live-champion
   comparison.
7. **S6 is a broad ballot expansion, not a rare exception.** On disjoint DEV
   seeds it added a move on 93.4% of natural leads. That meets the coverage
   request but may nearly double lead-search work; only the reviewed preflight
   can determine whether the full experiment is computationally sensible.
8. **S6's broadness is mostly generic whole-holding evacuation.** In bounded
   exact endgames those actions never beat the best existing live-ballot move
   in 128 balanced states. Boss/near bundles at least broke even directionally.
   The actionable split is “always source” versus “selectively spend search,”
   not removing shuai-pai from the ballot altogether.
9. **Pair-cap opportunities were small in count but stable at high N.** The
   repaired live diagnostic found eight such public-proof states, and all eight
   favored the low pair at every 300/1,000/3,000-world checkpoint. That is a
   strong mechanism clue, but shared rooms and multiple states per round make
   it unsuitable as a confidence interval or whole-game strength claim.
10. **Ballot visibility and search spend do not have to be the same thing.**
    S6 can keep all shuai-pai candidates inspectable while reserving a second
    expensive search for the 12.6% of broad triggers in the only stratum with
    positive exact evidence. That converts the broadness problem into a
    testable policy gate instead of deleting requested action coverage.
11. **A complete round does not always contain 100 play actions.** Singles do,
    but pairs, tractors and throws consume multiple cards at once. Catching
    this before packet review prevented a valid preflight from falsely
    refusing after hours of work; future whole-round validators must check
    complete four-seat trick groups rather than assume 25 single-card tricks.
12. **The broader pair-cap proof adds dose, but not monotonically.** Across 192
    natural roots it created two choices that v1 did not, but also undid one v1
    choice back to the champion. That is exactly why the next evidence must be
    complete-round treatment/null utility rather than counting activations as
    wins.
13. **Action count is not round completeness.** The first pair repair correctly
    allowed multi-card tricks but still accepted an impossible 84-card fixture.
    The durable contract is physical: 100 played cards, exactly 25 per seat,
    equal-width cyclic tricks and no card above two-deck multiplicity. When the
    flaw surfaced after admission, the run stopped at 1/4 and published no
    result; the consumed admission and progress line were preserved.
14. **Boss/near is cheap but not yet useful.** Its 87.37% search reduction did
    not retain the hoped-for DEV utility: 11 overrides tied and one lost two
    levels. This argues for diagnosing trigger quality and the losing witness,
    not spending immediately on a large confirmation or deleting broad S6.
15. **Exploration should preserve partial learning.** The S6 pilot retained
    completed rows only in memory until terminal publication. Future reusable
    DEV drivers should journal completed clusters, while confirmatory verdicts
    may still remain all-or-nothing and fail closed.
16. **The narrow S6 risk is failed-throw tail behavior, not every throw.** The
    only utility-changing override attempted `S4 S4 S6 S6`; the true opponents
    beat the bundle, so the engine forced only `S4 S4`, and attackers crossed
    from 75 to 100 points. Publicly proven all-boss gating removes that witness,
    but its ten observed overrides were all neutral. It is a safety refinement,
    not yet a strength hypothesis worth a large run.
17. **Pair-aware v1 changes often survive fresh high-N repricing, but not all
    metrics agree.** On all nine selected changes, level utility favored v1 on
    5–6 roots under two continuation models, opposed it on 1–2 and left two
    unresolved. One move gained roughly two points while losing roughly 0.13
    levels. Future screens must gate on level utility; points stay diagnostic.
18. **Raw pair v2 should protect v1 rather than replace it.** Its three
    differences from v1 contained two useful additions and one reversion that
    undid a roughly three-point v1 advantage. This is a composition problem,
    not evidence that broader pair tracking is uniformly better.
19. **S6's mismatch is not fixed by the existing level objective.** On the
    exact same report worlds, level-bracket scoring rejected seven neutral
    throws but still accepted the only two-level loss. It retained no wins.
    That closes a cheap full-pilot successor and points back to evaluator/search
    quality rather than another threshold on the same selected actions.
20. **The pair screen is now powered for the effect that motivated it.** A
    2,048-cluster run would be cheap but weak. The fixed 7,168 design is the
    largest one under both reviewed caps and has roughly 84% planning power at
    `+0.05`; its 80% minimum detectable effect is `0.04699` levels per round.
21. **Freeze authority and run authority remain separate.** The capacity PASS
    was enough to produce a byte-pinned 7,168-cluster proposal, not to spend
    it. Because the review prose also called 2,048 the bound, the next reviewer
    must explicitly accept or reject the exact powered size before gameplay.
22. **S6's value is concentrated in a sharper late-game shape.** The first
    post-hoc four-card data suggested full-hand boss/near throws, not partial
    bundles, carried the wins. A disjoint 128-state replication confirmed real
    exact action-set value: `+0.234` levels with LCB `+0.100`. The next problem
    is no longer sourcing; it is building an actor-information evaluator that
    can recognize those wins without seeing opponents' hands.
23. **The live search can recognize the full-hand opportunity, at least on the
    fitting population.** Four independent report-LCB streams per public state
    produced a `+0.307` exact level gain over the incumbent with LCB `+0.175`.
    The selector was not perfect—20 overrides were harmful—but beneficial
    overrides outnumbered them five to one and both roles stayed positive.
    Because these are the same opened states that selected the hypothesis,
    this clears fresh whole-game *design*, not strength.
24. **The selective S6 dose is neither vanishing nor broad.** It appeared in
    2.02% of 50,000 natural rounds and only in mid/late play. That is sparse
    enough to avoid the broad source's near-double-search cost, but frequent
    enough for a powered whole-game experiment if the capacity preflight
    confirms runtime and trigger counts.
25. **Bury strength is a post-bury hand-shape problem.** Point-bearing buries
    are a useful diagnostic stratum, but a strong ballot should explicitly
    include every feasible suit void and diverse candidates that preserve
    pairs, tractors, trump control and legal shuai-pai. MC must then evaluate
    those shapes with a continuation ballot capable of playing the preserved
    throw; otherwise it systematically undervalues the reason for the bury.
26. **A negative process filter is not fleet evidence.** A probe searched for
    `s6_full_hand` rather than the actual `s6_throw_full_hand...` command and
    falsely reported a healthy Air census idle. The original eight workers had
    remained detached and their logs were advancing. Eight unnecessary
    duplicate workers ran for roughly one minute before exact PID/namespace
    reconciliation stopped only them. No scored data or result was affected;
    [INC-12](../incidents/INC-20260812-12-fleet-monitor-false-negative.md)
    records the commands, impact and new absence-proof rule.
27. **Planning rates must name the estimator unit.** S6's source census counts
    one all-champion four-seat round, while its strength estimator sums two
    mirrored rounds per seed. The repaired packet now shows that treatment
    occupies all four seats once across those flips, caps multiple triggers at
    one affected cluster for planning, assumes no post-trigger trajectory
    equivalence, and makes measured run telemetry authoritative. This turns a
    plausible multiplication into a reviewable approximation.
28. **Fleet identity authority does not grant evidence-content authority.** A
    broad process inventory may read commands, working directories, file names,
    sizes and mtimes. The repaired monitor now tails only two exact reviewed
    score-free supervisor streams; every other recent log is metadata-only.
    This closes the sealed-content overreach found during the INC-12 repair.
29. **Joint bury exploration needs more than candidate zero as a reference.**
    The live ballot's first proposal is not the production bot's final searched
    lead. PR #51 therefore reports three explicitly descriptive optima: the
    existing lead menu under the incumbent bury, the pair/S6-widened menu under
    that same bury, and the full bury-plus-lead menu. This separates menu value
    from hand-shape value without pretending either raw winner is deployment
    evidence.
30. **“Include shuai-pai” must name when it can be played.** The first combo
    draft prices a throw attempted immediately after the bury; it cannot value
    one preserved for a later lead. PR #54 makes that difference measurable
    with separate baseline, public all-boss and boss-or-near continuation
    modes. They never call MC recursively, and each reusable journal namespace
    binds its mode and observed trigger dose, so later-throw value cannot be
    silently attributed to bury sourcing.
31. **Host pinning is also a scheduling constraint.** Air's long pair-aware
    screen could have left Mini idle after T4 even though S4 code was ready,
    because both pending S4 and S6 contracts named Air exactly. PR #53 prepares
    a separately reviewable Mini identity without changing S4's scientific
    design or borrowing the old Air marker. Exact host binding remains useful;
    the fix is to prepare the likely next host before it becomes free.
32. **Exploration should fail partially, not forget completely.** The first
    bury scorer preserved underfilled worlds inside one state but had no
    durable multi-state driver. PR #52 now writes each completed DEV state as
    an immutable record under one exact manifest. This is deliberately less
    strict than confirmatory evidence—it preserves learning and supports
    resume—but it still prevents mixing changed code, runtime or work settings.
